# Accrual Policy

**Owner:** Corporate Accounting
**Applies to:** All NorthPeak entities
**Effective:** 2026-01-01 · Version 1.0

## 1. Purpose

This policy ensures that expenses are recorded in the period in which the
goods or services were received, even when the vendor invoice has not yet
arrived or been paid. It governs how accruals are submitted, estimated,
booked, and reversed during the monthly close.

## 2. Scope

- All operating expenses incurred but not invoiced, or invoiced but not yet
  processed, at period end.
- Accruals are recorded in account **2400 - Accrued Expenses**.
- Capital expenditures, payroll, and tax accruals are governed by their own
  policies and are out of scope here.

## 3. Submission Requirements

- Each department submits an accrual listing to Corporate Accounting by
  **6:00 PM CT on business day 2** of the close.
- Each line item states: vendor, description of goods or services, period of
  service, and amount (actual or estimated, with the basis of the estimate).
- Individual items of **$250,000 or more** must be accrued. Smaller items may
  be accrued at the department's discretion if omitting them would distort
  the period's results.

## 4. Invoices That Arrive After Cutoff (Received, Not Invoiced)

When goods or services were received on or before period end but the invoice
has not arrived by the submission cutoff, the cost is **still accrued in the
period the service was received**, at the best available estimate, in this
order of preference:

1. The purchase order or contract amount.
2. The engagement letter or statement of work amount.
3. The vendor's quote or the prior period's recurring amount.

Estimates are trued up when the invoice arrives; differences flow through
the current period. **A missing invoice is never a reason to omit an
accrual.**

If a department fails to submit its listing by cutoff, Corporate Accounting
books the accrual from the underlying commitment documents (PO, contract, or
engagement letter) and records the omission in the close log. Repeated
omissions are escalated to the entity controller.

## 5. Reversals

- **All accruals in account 2400 are reversing accruals.** Each accrual
  reverses in full on the first business day of the following period.
- Non-reversing treatment is an exception and requires written Controller
  approval, with the justification documented on the journal.
- Before the close is finalized, Corporate Accounting reviews the 2400
  balance. **Any balance remaining after reversal day is investigated and
  resolved before the books close** - a residual balance normally means an
  accrual failed to reverse.
- If an invoice for an accrued service is paid while its accrual is still
  open, the cost is double-counted. The correction is made in the current
  period by clearing the accrual; the prior period is not restated for
  operational errors of this type.

## 6. Payment Matching Control

Before paying an invoice for services rendered in a prior period, Accounts
Payable checks for an open accrual for the same vendor and service. If one
exists, the payment is applied against the accrual rather than expensed a
second time.

## 7. Roles

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Department owners | Submit complete, supported accrual listings by cutoff |
| Corporate Accounting | Book accruals and estimates, run the reversal, review the 2400 balance, maintain the close log |
| Entity controllers | Resolve escalations and approve non-reversing exceptions |
| Accounts Payable | Match prior-period invoice payments against open accruals |
